CardKeeper Privacy Policy

Version 2026-11-03 · Effective 3 November 2026

CardKeeper is in development. This policy describes how the app handles data today. Before public launch it will be updated with the operating company's details and reviewed, and we will tell users of any change.

CardKeeper is an app for keeping and sharing business cards with control over who sees what. This policy explains what personal data we collect, why, who we share it with, how long we keep it, and the rights you have. It applies to the CardKeeper apps for Android and iOS and to the CardKeeper service and web pages.

We wrote it to meet the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018, India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 (DPDP Act), US state privacy laws including the California Consumer Privacy Act as amended by the CPRA, Brazil's LGPD, Canada's PIPEDA, Australia's Privacy Act 1988, Singapore's PDPA and other laws that give you similar rights. Where a law gives you more protection than this policy, that law applies.

Questions or requests: email vijayshankars@gmail.com. You can also see, change, export and delete your data, and withdraw consent, inside the app under Settings → Your choices.

2. Data we collect

Data you give us

  • Account: name, email address and/or phone number, country, the terms version you accepted and the choices you made (consents).
  • Your cards: what you put on them: name, job title, company, email addresses, phone numbers, website, address, social links, logo and images, photos of your paper card, services, prices and opening hours, skills, availability and meeting times, licence details for regulated professions, and similar.
  • Sensitive identifiers: we refuse full PAN, Aadhaar and similar national ID numbers on cards; only masked values (for example XXXXX1234X) can be stored. Values you mark as personal are encrypted at rest and masked by default.
  • Contacts and cards you receive or import: cards other people share with you, and contacts you choose to import from your phone, a file, Google or Microsoft. Nothing is imported until you pick it.
  • Things you create: notes, call logs, leads and follow-ups, opportunity posts (text and, if you choose, an audio clip), offers and coupons, appointments, referral offers and introductions.
  • Payout and payment details (if you use payments): see Payments.
  • Messages to us: what you send when you contact support.

Data we collect automatically

  • Sharing records: who you shared each card with, which details each person received, the mode (view only, locked, unlocked), allowed uses (business, marketing), expiry, views, locks and revocations. This is what lets you control and withdraw what you shared.
  • Security and audit logs: sign-ins, device approvals, reveals of masked details, and changes to your cards, with time stamps.
  • Device and technical data: app version, device type and operating system, IP address (used for security and abuse prevention), and crash information.
  • Public keys: each phone makes a signing key; we keep only the public part, to check that messages such as "lock this card" really came from you.
  • Advertising identifiers: when ads are shown, Google AdMob may collect your device's advertising ID and related data; see Advertising.

Data we do not collect

  • Your phone's calendar does not leave your phone (see Phone permissions).
  • Your location is not tracked in the background, and "near me" searches use a rounded position that is not stored.
  • We do not sell your personal data and do not "share" it for cross-context behavioural advertising as defined by California law, except as described under Advertising, which you can turn off.

5. Your control over sharing

  • You choose, for each person, which details they get (shown or masked), whether they may pass the card on, whether business or marketing use is allowed, and how long they have it.
  • You can lock, revoke or let a share expire at any time, and see who passed your card to whom.
  • Cards can be disabled (sharing paused) and archived; they are not lost by accident.
  • Your card is found in search only if you allow it, and only in the way you choose.

Honest limits: masking, view-only and locks control what the CardKeeper app shows and what our servers send. They cannot stop someone photographing their screen or writing a detail down, and a modified app could show what it receives. Digital signatures prove a card was not changed and came from the same key; they do not prove who someone is.

7. Advertising

CardKeeper shows ads from Google AdMob: a banner on the Cards screen, and on the free Personal plan a short ad before the first share, search or post of the day. Ads never appear on cards you share, and advertisers never receive your cards or contacts.

  • Where the law requires it (for example in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, and certain US states), Google's consent form asks before ads are personalised. You can change your choice at any time in Settings → Your choices.
  • You can also reset or limit your advertising ID in your phone's settings (Android: Privacy → Ads; iPhone: Privacy & Security → Tracking). On iPhone we do not track you across other companies' apps without your permission under Apple's App Tracking Transparency.
  • Google's use of data is described at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

Ad views on a card's own video ads are counted only as daily totals, only with the viewer's consent, and without recording who watched.

8. Payments

Referral fees can be paid through Razorpay (India) or Stripe. The money goes straight from the professional to the referrer's account at the provider; CardKeeper never holds it.

  • To receive fees, the referrer sets up a payout account. The provider checks their identity (know-your-customer checks). With Razorpay, your PAN, bank account and address go directly to Razorpay; we keep only masked values (for example the last four digits). With Stripe, Stripe collects the details on its own pages.
  • Card and UPI details are entered on the provider's page; we never see or store them.
  • We keep payment records, receipts and invoices (amounts, dates, the parties' names) as tax and accounting law requires.
  • Razorpay's and Stripe's own privacy policies apply to what they collect.

9. AI features

AI fill-in, online card reading and audio transcription are optional and run only when you use them. What you send is used to give you the result; our providers are contractually barred from using it to train their models. You always see and can edit the result before anything is saved or posted.

10. International transfers

During development our servers are in India, and some providers process data in other countries, including the United States. When personal data leaves the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, we use safeguards the law accepts: adequacy decisions (including the EU–US Data Privacy Framework where the provider is certified), the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses, and the UK International Data Transfer Addendum. Transfers from India follow the DPDP Act and any countries it restricts. You can ask us for a copy of the safeguards.

12. Security

  • All traffic is encrypted (HTTPS/TLS).
  • Masked and personal values, precise locations and licence numbers are encrypted at rest; integration tokens and keys are kept in a secrets store, never in plain text.
  • Sign-in uses one-time codes; we store only hashes of session tokens and API keys.
  • Locks, permits and revocations must be signed by the owner's own device key.
  • Highly confidential cards need device approval (Face ID, fingerprint or passcode) and always expire.
  • Access to production data is limited to people who need it.

No system is perfectly secure. Keep your phone locked and tell us at once if you think your account has been misused.

13. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you have the right to:

  • Access your data and get a copy;
  • Correct data that is wrong or incomplete;
  • Delete your data ("erasure");
  • Port your data: receive it in a common, machine-readable format, or have it sent to another service;
  • Object to processing based on legitimate interests, and to direct marketing at any time;
  • Restrict processing while a complaint is checked;
  • Withdraw consent at any time, as easily as you gave it. This does not affect what was done before;
  • Opt out of the sale or sharing of personal data and of targeted advertising (we do not sell data; you can turn off personalised ads);
  • Not be discriminated against for using your rights;
  • Nominate someone to exercise your rights if you die or cannot act (India);
  • Complain to a data protection authority (see Contact and complaints).

How: most rights work directly in the app (Settings → Your choices: export, change choices, delete account). Or email vijayshankars@gmail.com. We may need to confirm it is you. We answer within one month (GDPR, UK GDPR), 45 days (US state laws), or the time your law sets, and tell you if we need longer. Requests are free unless they are clearly unfounded or excessive. An authorised agent may make a request for you with your signed permission.

14. Rights by region

European Economic Area, United Kingdom and Switzerland

All rights in section 13 apply. You may complain to the supervisory authority where you live or work, or where an issue happened. In the UK, that is the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk).

India

Under the DPDP Act you may access a summary of your data and who it was shared with, correct, complete, update or erase it, withdraw consent, nominate another person, and have your grievances addressed by our Grievance Officer (section 1). If you are not satisfied, you may complain to the Data Protection Board of India. You can also manage consent through a registered Consent Manager when available.

United States (California, Colorado, Connecticut, Virginia, Utah, Texas, Oregon and other states)

  • Categories collected in the last 12 months: identifiers (name, email, phone, IP address, advertising ID); customer records (card details); commercial information (plans, payments); internet activity (app use, ad interactions); approximate and, if you choose, precise geolocation; audio (only clips you attach); professional information (job, company, licences); inferences (none used for profiling). Sources, purposes and recipients are described above.
  • Sensitive personal information: precise geolocation (only when you share it), account sign-in data and financial account details for payouts. We use it only to provide the service you asked for, and not to infer characteristics about you.
  • Sale and sharing: we do not sell personal data. Personalised ads may count as "sharing" for cross-context behavioural advertising; you can opt out by declining personalised ads in Settings → Your choices or in your phone's ad settings.
  • Appeals: if we decline a request, you may appeal by replying to our answer; if we deny the appeal, you may contact your state Attorney General.

Brazil (LGPD)

You may confirm processing, access, correct, anonymise, block or delete unnecessary data, port your data, learn who we share it with, and withdraw consent. You may complain to the ANPD.

Canada (PIPEDA)

You may access and correct your data and withdraw consent, subject to legal limits. You may complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.

Australia

You may access and correct your data. If you are not satisfied with our answer to a complaint, you may contact the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC).

Singapore and elsewhere

You may access and correct your data and withdraw consent under the PDPA, and use the rights your local law gives you.

15. Children

CardKeeper is a professional networking tool and is not meant for children. You must be at least 18, or the age of digital consent where you live if higher, to use it. We do not knowingly collect personal data from children under 13 (or under 16 in the EEA, or under 18 in India without verifiable parental consent). If we learn we have, we delete it. Parents or guardians who think a child has given us data can contact us.

16. Data about other people

When you add or import someone else's contact details, or a card someone shared with you, you are responsible for having the right to do so and for using it only as allowed. The allowed uses each owner sets (business, marketing) are shown with every card, and CardKeeper holds back from your CRM what you may not send. If you are in someone's contacts in CardKeeper and want to know more, contact us.

17. Automated decisions

We do not make decisions about you based solely on automated processing that have legal or similarly significant effects. Search results and suggestions are ranked automatically, but you decide what to do with them.

18. Data breaches

If a breach puts your data at risk, we notify the relevant authorities and, where required, you, within the times the law sets (for example 72 hours to the supervisory authority under GDPR, and to the Data Protection Board and affected users under India's DPDP Act), and tell you what we are doing about it.

19. Changes to this policy

We will post any change here with a new version date. If a change is significant, we will tell you in the app before it takes effect and, where the law requires, ask for your consent again.

20. Contact and complaints

Email vijayshankars@gmail.com for any question, request or complaint about your personal data. We will try to resolve it with you first. You also have the right to complain to your data protection authority at any time.
